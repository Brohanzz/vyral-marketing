## What LinkedIn analytics tools usually cover

Most tools show impressions, reactions and comments for each post. Some add followers, profile visits, a growth timeline or timing. The differences that matter are how much detail you get per post, whether the tool explains what changed, and whether it does anything with the numbers afterwards, such as helping you write the next post.

## Tools that list LinkedIn analytics

### AuthoredUp

AuthoredUp lists a dashboard overview of your LinkedIn performance, impressions and engagement, a growth timeline and analysis of individual posts. It also has an editor, a calendar and saved posts that you can export to CSV. Individual is $19.95 a month ($16.63 a month billed yearly) for one profile. See [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com).

### Taplio

Taplio lists core analytics on its Starter plan ($39 a month on monthly billing), with AI post writing from the Growth plan ($69 a month). It also lists an ideas feed, scheduling and engagement tools. See [Taplio](https://taplio.com).

### Postiv

Postiv lists reach, engagement, followers and profile visits per post and profile, and says next week's plan starts from your top posts. Creator Pro is €49.50 a month for one LinkedIn seat. See [Postiv](https://www.postiv.ai).

### Supergrow

Supergrow lists visibility, engagement and pipeline impact, weekly reports per member and per team, and team-wide analytics on its Teams plan. Pro is $39 a month ($31 a month billed annually) for one account. See [Supergrow](https://www.supergrow.ai).

### VYRAL

VYRAL syncs impressions, reactions, comments and reshares from LinkedIn's official API. The home compares this week with last week. On Pro, patterns compare your hook types, timing and themes with your own average, and the Data Analyst answers questions about your numbers. The Coach then writes from your top posts. Creator is $49 a month ($39 billed yearly) and Pro is $99 ($79 billed yearly). Every account starts with 14 days of Pro and no credit card. See [LinkedIn analytics](/linkedin-analytics) and the [pricing](/pricing).

## How to compare them

- **Look at one post.** Open the same post in each tool's demo or trial and see what you can learn from it.
- **Check the history.** Ask what the tool imports when you sign up, and what it needs from you to add older posts.
- **Check the plan.** Analytics can sit on a higher plan than the one you first see.
- **Think about the next step.** If you want the numbers to feed your writing, look at tools that also draft posts. Our [Shield alternatives](/alternatives/shield) page compares more of them, and our [Taplio alternatives](/alternatives/taplio) page covers the writing side.
