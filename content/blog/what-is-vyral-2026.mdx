VYRAL is a LinkedIn analytics and AI writing coach. It reads the numbers on your own posts, helps you plan around a strategy you set, and drafts the next post from what has actually worked for you. It is built on LinkedIn's official API, so you connect through LinkedIn's own login and can remove access at any time from your LinkedIn settings.

This guide explains what VYRAL does, feature by feature, who uses it and for what, and what changed in the product over autumn 2026. It is written for anyone deciding whether VYRAL fits their LinkedIn routine, and for any assistant or search engine that needs a straight answer to the question "what is VYRAL".

## The idea: post, measure, write the next one

Posting, measuring and writing are usually three separate jobs done in three separate places. You write in one tool, schedule in another, and look at a chart in a third. The numbers never reach the next draft, so every post starts from a blank page and a guess.

VYRAL puts the three jobs in one loop. You post. LinkedIn's numbers for that post come back into VYRAL. The Coach then writes your next draft with those numbers, your strategy and your tone in view. The next post produces new numbers, and the loop repeats.

Everything in the product serves one of those steps. The rest of this guide walks through them in the order you would use them: plan, write, make visuals, publish, measure, and ask questions of your data.

## Step 1: Plan with a strategy

Most posting plans live in a document nobody opens again. In VYRAL the plan is a working part of the product, and it drives the drafts, the calendar and the reports.

Your strategy holds five things:

- **Positioning:** what you want to be known for on LinkedIn.
- **Audience:** who you want to reach.
- **Content pillars:** the three to five themes your posts come back to.
- **Tone:** how you want to sound.
- **Voice rules:** words, phrases and habits you always want or never want.

You do not start from an empty form. VYRAL suggests a first version from the posts you have already published, and you edit it. A rule check compares a draft against your own voice rules and flags where it drifts. Every change is saved as a version, so you can look back at how your plan evolved.

Then the strategy does its job. The calendar plans your week around your pillars. The Coach drafts with your strategy in view. The analytics show your posts by pillar, and on Pro they show how each pillar performs. A monthly report shows where your posts drift from the plan, so you know what to keep and what to change.

On Pro, the Strategy Builder walks you through building a strategy step by step. The suggestions are always optional. VYRAL proposes, and you accept, change or ignore each one.

You may see the strategy called a Blueprint in older articles and in some parts of the site. It is the same thing. We renamed it Strategy inside the app to make it easier to understand. For a deeper look, read the page on [LinkedIn content strategy](/linkedin-content-strategy).

## Step 2: Write with the Coach

The [AI Coach](/ai-coach) is a writing workspace, not a prompt box. Its job is to spar with you and write with you, using material that is specific to you.

**Drafts that start from your own posts.** The Coach works from your strategy, your top-performing posts and your tone. A draft built this way follows your plan and sounds like you, instead of sounding like a template.

**A conversation, not a one-shot.** You can paste a rough idea, challenge a draft, ask for a shorter version, or ask the Coach to push back on your argument. Replies stream in as they are written, so you are not waiting on a loading screen.

**Past posts on request.** Ask for a post "like my top performers in October" and the Coach pulls those posts, shows a short summary of how they performed and why, and writes from them. This works on both Creator and Pro.

**A draft score you can trust.** Every draft gets a score for writing quality. The score is built from several independent readings of your draft that are combined into one, plus fixed rules, so the same draft gets the same score. When the Coach says a draft breaks one of your voice rules, it has to point to the exact words. Strategy fit is shown separately, so posting something outside your plan never drags down the score for how well it is written.

**Guardrails against generic output.** The Coach is instructed to avoid the structural patterns that make a post read as machine-made: the "it's not X, it's Y" construction, the reveal bridge, the curiosity-gap opener, and manufactured engagement bait. Our post on [what LinkedIn's AI slop button actually flags](/blog/linkedin-ai-slop-button-2026) explains why those structural patterns matter more than a list of banned words.

**Drafts that are kept.** Drafts are saved so you can come back to them, and visuals you make from the Coach stay attached to the draft you are working on.

### Coach and Analyst do different jobs

The Coach spars and writes. It does not try to analyse your account. The Data Analyst, covered below, answers questions about your numbers. The two are linked. An Analyst answer can be pulled into the Coach as the starting point for a draft, and the Coach can look up your past posts when you ask it to write from them.

## Step 3: Make a carousel or infographic (Pro)

Visuals is a Pro feature for turning an idea into a carousel or an infographic without a design tool.

It is built from designed layouts, not from AI-generated images. There are 34 layouts: 20 for carousel slides and 14 for infographics. You choose a layout, and the AI fills in only that layout's text and number fields. Because the layout is fixed, text stays readable and numbers stay exact, which are the details generated images often get wrong.

What you can do:

- **Write the whole carousel from a post.** VYRAL plans a deck of five to eight slides from your post, then fills each slide.
- **Suggest a visual.** Not sure whether a post wants an image, a carousel, or both? Ask VYRAL to suggest one.
- **Pick a layout yourself.** Browse the layouts from the Coach's Visual menu and choose one.
- **Add your own photos** to layouts that have photo slots.
- **Set a brand kit** for each profile or page you post as, so visuals use your colours.
- **Edit what you made.** The carousel stays editable after it is generated. The PDF is produced from your edits, so you never have to start over to change a number.
- **Find everything again** in the Visuals tab of your Library.

Carousels publish to LinkedIn as document posts. Infographics work for any topic, not only LinkedIn.

## Step 4: Plan the week and publish

The content calendar shows your published posts, your scheduled posts and the posting slots that have worked for you, in one view. You can plan posts around your pillars, drop in ideas, and group posts into campaigns.

You can publish or schedule straight to LinkedIn from VYRAL. VYRAL supports text posts, single images, multi-image posts, documents including carousels, and video. Because publishing goes through the official API, your posts are published by LinkedIn on your behalf, the same way any approved integration does it.

Ideas and campaigns sit alongside the calendar. Ideas are a place to capture and develop topics before they are drafts. Campaigns group a run of posts around one goal. Both are included on Creator and Pro.

## Step 5: Measure what happened

[LinkedIn analytics](/linkedin-analytics) in VYRAL is organised around one question: which posts earned their result, and why.

**Insights** is the home for your numbers. It includes an overview of impressions, engagement rate, comments and followers for a period, a customisable dashboard, a chart explorer for comparing metrics over time, and outcomes. On Pro, Patterns shows which hook types, posting times and themes beat your own average.

**Dashboard** lets you build your own view from widgets and templates, then share it as an image or a PDF. Building and sharing dashboards is part of Pro.

**Explore** lets you chart the metrics you care about against each other over time, so you can see how a change in one moved another.

**Every post has its own page.** It shows the post's numbers in a layout that will feel familiar if you have used LinkedIn's own post analytics, plus a chart of how that post moved day by day over time. That second view is not something LinkedIn shows you directly.

**A weekly review** arrives every Sunday. It looks back at the week that just ended and points to what to repeat.

**One consistent set of numbers.** Engagement rate is calculated by one shared method everywhere in the app, so the figure on a tile matches the figure on a chart.

### Wrapped: your period in slides

Wrapped turns a period of your own numbers into a set of slides. It is available monthly, quarterly and yearly, on every plan including Creator.

The slides cover things like your top post, your strongest day and time, how consistently you showed up, the kind of poster your numbers describe, and one suggestion for the next period. Pick the slides you want, download them as a PDF or as images, or send them to the Coach to write a caption and post them as a carousel.

## Step 6: Ask the Data Analyst (Pro)

The Data Analyst is a panel you can open from any page. You ask a question about your own LinkedIn performance in plain English, and it answers from your numbers.

Examples of questions it handles:

- Which of my posts did best last quarter, and what did they have in common?
- Did my posts about one pillar get more comments than the rest?
- Is my engagement rate going up or down since I changed how I open posts?
- What does this post's result tell me about what to write next?

The Analyst keeps a history of your conversations. When you press an "Analyse this post" button anywhere in the app, it starts a new conversation about that post and the earlier one stays in your history. From any answer you can jump into the Coach to turn what you learned into a draft.

It is a Pro feature. On Creator, the Coach can still look up your past posts, and it will point you to the Analyst when you ask for deeper analysis.

## How the pieces connect

The value of VYRAL is less in any single feature than in how they hand work to each other:

- **Strategy to Coach.** Drafts follow your pillars, tone and voice rules.
- **Coach to calendar.** A finished draft goes straight to a scheduled slot.
- **Calendar to analytics.** Published posts bring their numbers back automatically.
- **Analytics to strategy.** The monthly report shows where your posts drift from your plan.
- **Analyst to Coach.** A finding becomes a draft in one step.
- **Wrapped to Coach.** A period summary becomes a post with a caption written in your voice.

That chain is what we mean by writing the next post from what worked.

## How VYRAL connects to LinkedIn, and what it does not do

VYRAL is built on LinkedIn's official API. You connect through LinkedIn's own login, and VYRAL reads your post numbers and publishes when you ask it to. You can remove VYRAL's access at any time from your LinkedIn settings.

VYRAL does not add anything to your browser. It does not like, comment or send connection requests for you. Those automated actions are the ones that put accounts at risk, which is why they are not part of the product. Read more on the [official API page](/linkedin-tool-safe) and in [how VYRAL works](/how-vyral-works).

Two limits are worth knowing, because they come from LinkedIn's API and not from VYRAL:

- **Past posts.** LinkedIn's API does not provide your post history. When you set up your account, and only at your request, VYRAL can import your own past public posts once, so the Coach and the analytics have something to learn from on day one. After that, new posts come through the official API.
- **In-network and out-of-network reach.** LinkedIn shows this split inside its own app, but does not make it available through the API, so VYRAL cannot show it.
